Cayley vs Schreier — why Gc(k,n) is not a Cayley graph

A Cayley graph has one vertex for every group element. A Schreier coset graph has one vertex for every coset. The graphs Gc(k,n) of binary words under consecutive c-cycles are the second kind, although they are often printed under the first name. Press START to watch the left graph fold onto the right one.

What you are looking at

The bead analogy

Thread n beads on a wire, each with its own label. The Cayley graph is a map with one room for every arrangement of those labeled beads, n! rooms in all, and a corridor wherever one allowed move (rotating c neighboring beads) turns one arrangement into another.

Now repaint the beads: k of them dark, the rest light. You can no longer tell the dark beads apart, nor the light ones. Blocks of k!(n−k)! rooms that differed only by which dark bead was where become a single room. Same moves, coarser map. That coarser map is the Schreier graph, and START performs the repainting.

Why the names differ

A Cayley graph Cay(G, S) has the elements of G as vertices. A Schreier graph has the cosets of a subgroup H as vertices; here H = Stab(0k1n−k) = Sk × Sn−k. The two notions coincide only when H is trivial, so that every coset is a single element.

A test you can apply by eye

A Cayley graph is always vertex-transitive, hence regular, and it is connected exactly when the generators generate the whole group. A Schreier graph need be neither regular nor vertex-transitive. Try c = 4, n = 4, k = 2: the right panel has vertices of degree 1 and of degree 2, which no Cayley graph can have.

What the collapse costs

Merged rooms mean merged corridors. A move that only swaps beads of the same color stays inside one room and becomes a loop, invisible on the coarse map. Distances on the coarse map can therefore only shrink, which is why a lower bound derived from the Schreier graph can sit strictly below the true distance in the Cayley graph.

Controls

START runs the collapse. RESET rebuilds both layouts from the same seed. Hovering a vertex in either panel outlines its whole fiber on the left and the matching coset on the right.
